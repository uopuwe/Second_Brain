If **the skin around the red spot hurts**, that's a more important clue than the spot itself. Pain suggests inflammation rather than just a harmless color change.

Some possibilities include:

- 🌞 **A localized sunburn**, especially if the area is tender when you touch it.
- 🦟 **An insect bite or sting**, which can cause pain before it becomes itchy.
- 🦠 **An early skin infection (cellulitis)**, particularly if the redness is spreading, feels warm, and the pain is increasing.
- 🩸 **A small bruise or ruptured blood vessel** from minor unnoticed trauma, which can be tender.

### What to check right now
- Is the spot **warm** compared with the surrounding skin?
- Is the redness **getting larger**?
- Is it **raised** or flat?
- Does it **blanch** (turn white briefly) when you press on it?
- Do you have **fever, chills, or red streaks** extending from it?

### What you should do
- Keep the area out of the sun.
- Apply a **cool compress** for 10-15 minutes at a time.
- If it's sore, you can take an over-the-counter pain reliever such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen if it's safe for you to use.
- Avoid scratching or squeezing the area.
